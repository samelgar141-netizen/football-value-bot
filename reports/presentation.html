The Problem

  • Bookmakers build a systematic margin (vig) into every market — typically 5–8%
  • Traditional bettors rely on gut feel, form guides, or tipsters — largely random outcomes
  • The gap: if a bookmaker prices a home win at 2/1 (33% implied) but the true probability is 45%, that’s a mispricing worth exploiting repeatedly
  • Over a large sample, positive expected value (EV) compounds into profit
Example: 100 bets at +20% EV, £2 average stake
Expected return: +£40 on £200 staked
The bookmaker margin means that if you bet randomly, you lose around 5-8% of your stake over time. The only way to beat this long-term is to have a model that is more accurate than the bookmaker's pricing. When our model assigns a higher probability to an outcome than the bookmaker's odds imply, we have an edge. The key insight is that this edge, applied systematically over many bets, produces a profit.

System Architecture

  • Pure Python, no database — all storage in CSV files
  • Two free-tier APIs: football-data.org (results + fixtures) and the-odds-api.com (bookmaker odds)
  • Six modules: fetch → model → detect → report → ledger → dashboard
  • Single entry point: python run_weekly.py
  • Entire pipeline runs in ~15 seconds once a week
The system is deliberately simple -- no database, no web server, no cloud dependencies. Everything runs locally and stores data in CSV files. Two free APIs provide all the data we need: match results and bookmaker odds. The entire system runs in about 15 seconds. Run it on a Sunday morning before the fixtures kick off.

Why Poisson?

  • Football goals are rare, independent events — a textbook Poisson process
  • The Poisson formula gives P(k goals) given average rate λ:
P(k) = (e−λ × λk) / k!
  • The only raw input is goals — λ is derived entirely from each team’s historical goals scored and conceded
  • Build an 11×11 score matrix: every scoreline 0-0 to 10-10 with its probability
  • Sum cells to derive any market probability — home win, draw, over 2.5, BTTS, etc.

Poisson is the right model for football because goals satisfy the core assumptions: they're rare (average ~2.5 per game), roughly independent of each other, and the rate is relatively stable within a game. The critical point for this project: goals are the ONLY raw data input right now. All the sophistication comes from how we process those goals, not from adding new data sources. The score matrix is the engine -- once we have it, we can calculate any market probability by summing the right cells.

Model Features (Current)

All 4 features are transformations of a single raw signal: goals

FeatureWhat it doesWhy it matters
Attack / Defence ratings Goals scored/conceded as ratio to league average Normalises for teams of different strength
Exponential decay Recent matches count more (half-life ~139 days) Captures form, manager changes, injuries
Regression to mean Raw ratings shrunk toward 1.0 by sample size Prevents extreme ratings from small samples
Dixon-Coles correction Adjusts 0-0, 1-0, 0-1, 1-1 cell probabilities (ρ = −0.10) Fixes Poisson’s known underestimation of low-scoring draws
The model is deliberately conservative. We're using only goals data, but we apply four layers of statistical sophistication. Exponential decay is important because a manager change or a key injury can shift a team's performance level overnight. We don't want results from 9 months ago to carry equal weight. Dixon-Coles is a known fix for a known Poisson limitation: it slightly underestimates the frequency of 0-0 and 1-1 draws. The correction adjusts those specific scoreline probabilities. The only raw input is goals. This is intentional -- it's a solid baseline before adding richer inputs.

From Score Matrix to Markets

  • The 11×11 matrix has a probability for every scoreline
  • Markets derived by summing the right cells:

Home winLower triangle (home > away)
DrawDiagonal (home = away)

Under 2.5All cells where total ≤ 2

10 fixtures × 6 markets = 60 betting opportunities evaluated each week 


def predict_match(home, away, stats):
    lambda_home = (
        stats.loc[home, 'home_attack']
        * stats.loc[away, 'away_defence']
        * league_avg_home
    )
    lambda_away = (
        stats.loc[away, 'away_attack']
        * stats.loc[home, 'home_defence']
        * league_avg_away
    )

    home_probs = poisson.pmf(range(11), lambda_home)
    away_probs = poisson.pmf(range(11), lambda_away)

    # 11x11 score matrix
    matrix = np.outer(home_probs, away_probs)

    # Dixon-Coles correction on low scores
    matrix = _dixon_coles(matrix, lambda_home, lambda_away)

    return matrix
The np.outer call is the heart of the model. It takes two probability distributions -- home goals and away goals -- and produces a joint distribution over all possible scorelines. This works because Poisson assumes independence between the two teams' scoring. The Dixon-Coles correction partially relaxes this assumption for low-scoring games. From this single matrix, we get all six market probabilities. Summing the right cells is all it takes.

Finding Value: EV and Vig Removal

Step 1 — Remove bookmaker margin

Raw implied prob = 1 / decimal_odds
True prob = raw_prob / (sum of all raw probs in market)
1X2 market odds: 2.30 / 3.40 / 3.20  →  overround = 107.3%
True home prob = (1/2.30) / 1.073 = 40.5% (not 43.5%)

Step 2 — Calculate Expected Value

EV = (Model probability × Decimal odds) − 1
Model: 60% home win  ·  Bookmaker: 2.10  →  EV = (0.60 × 2.10) − 1 = +26%

Only bets with EV > 20% are surfaced — configurable via MIN_EV_THRESHOLD in config.py

Vig removal is essential. Without it we'd be comparing our model probability against an artificially inflated bookmaker probability, which understates our edge. Step 1 normalises the bookmaker's prices to sum to exactly 100%, removing their margin. This gives us the true implied probability. Step 2 is then simple: if our model says the probability is higher than the true implied probability, and the difference is large enough to overcome the margin, we have a value bet. The 20% threshold was chosen to ensure we only bet when we have a meaningful edge -- not just statistical noise.

Stake Sizing: Kelly Criterion

  • Kelly: mathematically optimal fraction of bankroll for maximum long-run growth
f = (b×p − q) / b
b = odds−1  ·  p = model prob  ·  q = 1−p
  • Problem: Full Kelly is aggressive — large drawdowns
  • Our approach: 25% of full Kelly (fractional Kelly)
GW38 example: Tottenham vs Everton away

Model prob: 49.8%  ·  Odds: 4.0 (3/1)  ·  EV: +99%

Full Kelly = 33.0% of bankroll
Fractional (25%) = 8.25%
Bankroll £20 → Stake = £1.65

Each bet’s stake auto-calculated and shown in the Upcoming Bets tab of the dashboard

Kelly gives you the exact fraction of your bankroll to bet on any given opportunity to maximise long-run wealth. But full Kelly is aggressive -- a string of losses early can wipe out a large percentage of the bankroll. Fractional Kelly at 25% gives up some theoretical return in exchange for a much smoother bankroll curve. In practice the stakes are small because we're working with a £20 starting bankroll to test the model -- the system scales linearly with bankroll size.

Elo ratings solve the cold-start problem -- early in the season we have few data points, so attack/defence ratings are noisy. Elo from previous seasons gives a prior that decays as new evidence arrives. H2H data captures systematic patterns in specific matchups -- some teams persistently under- or over-perform against particular opponents. Line movement tracking is interesting: if a line moves significantly between Friday and Sunday morning, it often signals sharp money (professional bettors) taking a position. This could be used as a signal filter on top of our EV calculation. None of these require new APIs -- all free or already available.
