Welcome. This presentation walks through a fully automated football value betting pipeline built in Python. The Problem

  • Bookmakers build a systematic margin (vig) into every market — typically 5–8%
  • Traditional bettors rely on gut feel, form guides, or tipsters — largely random outcomes
  • The gap: if a bookmaker prices a home win at 2/1 (33% implied) but the true probability is 45%, that’s a mispricing worth exploiting repeatedly
  • Bookmakers also shift odds based on volume of money placed — not just probability changes. Heavy one-sided betting moves the line, creating mispricings independent of the true probability
  • Over a large sample, positive expected value (EV) compounds into profit
Example: 100 bets at +20% EV, £2 average stake
Expected return: +£40 on £200 staked

value_bets_latest.csv — GW38 bets with model probability, EV, and bookmaker columns

The bookmaker margin means that if you bet randomly, you lose around 5-8% of your stake over time. The only way to beat this long-term is to have a model that is more accurate than the bookmaker's pricing. When our model assigns a higher probability to an outcome than the bookmaker's odds imply, we have an edge. The key insight is that this edge, applied systematically over many bets, produces a profit.

Why Poisson?

  • Football goals are rare, independent events — a textbook Poisson process
  • The Poisson formula gives P(k goals) given average rate λ:
P(k) = (e−λ × λk) / k!
  • The only raw input is goals — λ is derived entirely from each team’s historical goals scored and conceded
  • Build an 11×11 score matrix: every scoreline 0-0 to 10-10 with its probability
  • Sum cells to derive any market probability — home win, draw, over 2.5, BTTS, etc.

README.md worked example — Arsenal vs Man City score matrix

Poisson is the right model for football because goals satisfy the core assumptions: they're rare (average ~2.5 per game), roughly independent of each other, and the rate is relatively stable within a game. The critical point for this project: goals are the ONLY raw data input right now. All the sophistication comes from how we process those goals, not from adding new data sources. The score matrix is the engine -- once we have it, we can calculate any market probability by summing the right cells. Next slide walks through a concrete λ calculation using real team_stats.csv values.

The 0.82 home_attack means Tottenham score 18% fewer goals at home than the league average -- a below-average home attack that season. The 0.89 away_defence for Everton means they concede 11% fewer away goals than average -- a decent defensive record away from home. λ = 0.82 × 0.89 × 1.52 = 1.11. With λ = 1.11, there's a 33% chance Tottenham score 0, 36.6% they score 1, 20.3% they score 2. The most likely outcome is 1 goal. Combined with Everton's away attack (1.06) and Tottenham's home defence (1.27), λ_away = 1.64 — and summing the upper-triangle cells of the score matrix gives Everton a 49.8% away win probability, shown on the next slide.

Model Features (Current)

All 4 features are transformations of a single raw signal: goals

FeatureWhat it doesWhy it matters
Attack / Defence ratings Goals scored/conceded as ratio to league average Normalises for teams of different strength
Exponential decay Recent matches count more (half-life ~139 days) Captures form, manager changes, injuries
Regression to mean Raw ratings shrunk toward 1.0 by sample size Prevents extreme ratings from small samples
Dixon-Coles correction Adjusts 0-0, 1-0, 0-1, 1-1 cell probabilities (ρ = −0.10) Fixes Poisson’s known underestimation of low-scoring draws

data/processed/team_stats.csv — attack & defence ratings for all 20 PL teams

The model is deliberately conservative. We're using only goals data, but we apply four layers of statistical sophistication. Exponential decay is important because a manager change or a key injury can shift a team's performance level overnight. We don't want results from 9 months ago to carry equal weight. Dixon-Coles is a known fix for a known Poisson limitation: it slightly underestimates the frequency of 0-0 and 1-1 draws. The correction adjusts those specific scoreline probabilities. The only raw input is goals. This is intentional -- it's a solid baseline before adding richer inputs.

def predict_match(home, away, stats):
    lambda_home = (
        stats.loc[home, 'home_attack']
        * stats.loc[away, 'away_defence']
        * league_avg_home
    )
    lambda_away = (
        stats.loc[away, 'away_attack']
        * stats.loc[home, 'home_defence']
        * league_avg_away
    )

    home_probs = poisson.pmf(range(11), lambda_home)
    away_probs = poisson.pmf(range(11), lambda_away)

    # 11x11 score matrix
    matrix = np.outer(home_probs, away_probs)

    # Dixon-Coles correction on low scores
    matrix = _dixon_coles(matrix, lambda_home, lambda_away)

    return matrix

README.md — Arsenal vs Man City score matrix output

The np.outer call is the heart of the model. It takes two probability distributions -- home goals and away goals -- and produces a joint distribution over all possible scorelines. This works because Poisson assumes independence between the two teams' scoring. The Dixon-Coles correction partially relaxes this assumption for low-scoring games. From this single matrix, we get all six market probabilities. Summing the right cells is all it takes.

The most likely single scoreline is 1-1 at 11.6%, but the most likely Everton win scoreline is 0-1 at 10.5% — roughly a 1-in-10 chance of that exact result. This 49.8% model probability feeds directly into the EV calculation on the next two slides.

Finding Value: EV and Vig Removal

Example: Tottenham vs Everton — GW38 away market (Bet365)

Step 1 — Remove bookmaker margin

Raw implied prob = 1 / decimal_odds  ·  True prob = raw_prob ÷ overround
Overround = sum of all raw implied probs in the market — always > 100%, the bookmaker’s built-in margin
Tottenham (home)
odds: 1.85
raw: 1/1.85 = 54.1%
Draw
odds: 3.50
raw: 1/3.50 = 28.6%
Everton (away)
odds: 4.00
raw: 1/4.00 = 25.0%
Overround = 54.1 + 28.6 + 25.0 = 107.6%  →  bookmaker margin = 7.6%
Vig-removed Everton away prob = 25.0 ÷ 107.6 = 23.2%
Vig removal is essential. Without it we'd be comparing our model probability against an artificially inflated bookmaker probability. The overround tells you the bookmaker's total margin across the whole market. Here it's 107.6% — meaning for every £107.60 bet proportionally, the bookmaker expects to pay out £100. Their edge is £7.60. Step 1 divides each raw implied probability by the overround (107.6%), which normalises the market to sum to 100%. This removes the margin and gives us the true implied probability: 23.2% for Everton to win away. These odds (1.85 / 3.50 / 4.00) were the best available on Bet365 for this market. The pipeline pulls from up to 8 bookmakers and takes the best odds for each outcome.

Finding Value: EV and Vig Removal

Example: Tottenham vs Everton — GW38 away market (Bet365)  ·  Step 1 result: vig-removed Everton away prob = 23.2%

Step 2 — Calculate Expected Value

EV = (Model probability × Decimal odds) − 1
Model prob for Everton away: 49.8%  ·  Bet365 decimal odds: 4.00
EV = (0.498 × 4.00) − 1 = +99%
Model sees 49.8% — market prices only 23.2% → clear value

Only bets with EV > 20% are surfaced — configurable via MIN_EV_THRESHOLD in config.py

Step 2 is straightforward: our model gives Everton a 49.8% chance of winning away — more than double the market's vig-removed 23.2%. The 49.8% came from summing the upper-triangle cells of the score matrix on the previous slide. The EV of +99% means for every £1 staked, we expect to return £1.99 long-run. This is an unusually large edge — in practice most value bets are in the 20-50% EV range. The 20% EV threshold filters out marginal edges and model noise. Only bets where we have a meaningful, statistically significant edge are surfaced.

Stake Sizing: Kelly Criterion

  • Kelly: mathematically optimal fraction of bankroll for maximum long-run growth
f = (b×p − q) / b
b = odds−1  ·  p = model prob  ·  q = 1−p
  • Problem: Full Kelly is aggressive — large drawdowns
  • Our approach: 25% of full Kelly (fractional Kelly)
GW38 example: Tottenham vs Everton away

Model prob: 49.8%  ·  Odds: 4.0 (3/1)  ·  EV: +99%

Full Kelly = 33.0% of bankroll
Fractional (25%) = 8.25%
Bankroll £20 → Stake = £1.65

Each bet’s stake auto-calculated and shown in the Upcoming Bets tab of the dashboard

Kelly gives you the exact fraction of your bankroll to bet on any given opportunity to maximise long-run wealth. But full Kelly is aggressive -- a string of losses early can wipe out a large percentage of the bankroll. Fractional Kelly at 25% gives up some theoretical return in exchange for a much smoother bankroll curve. In practice the stakes are small because we're working with a £20 starting bankroll to test the model -- the system scales linearly with bankroll size.

Easiest win: Shots on target is already in the football-data.org match response — it’s downloaded every week but not saved. One column added to fetch_results() unlocks it at zero cost. xG requires a second free source (Understat) but gives the biggest accuracy improvement.
This slide answers a key question: what do we need to do to improve the model? Shots on target is the lowest-hanging fruit. The data is already arriving in our API response -- we just discard it. Adding one column to the pipeline gives us a quality proxy for chance creation. xG is the gold standard input for Poisson football models. It removes the noise of finishing luck. A striker who hits the post three times in a game creates the same goals as one who scores zero -- xG captures the difference. Possession on its own is a weak predictor -- some of the best counter-attacking teams in the world have low possession but win regularly.
